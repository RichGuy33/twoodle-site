Long-Distance Anniversary Ideas: 12 Ways to Celebrate Apart

By the Twoodle team · November 2026

Disclosure: we make Twoodle, a couples widget app for iPhone and Android. Most ideas here need nothing but a phone and a little planning, and we say so where an app helps.

Nobody warns you about the first anniversary you spend in two different time zones. You're supposed to be clinking glasses. Instead you're staring at a calendar reminder that says "Anniversary!" while your partner is, at that exact moment, asleep.

Good news: an anniversary is not a location, it's a moment you both agree matters. Here are twelve ways to mark it apart, sorted by budget and effort, plus a tiny plan so the day doesn't sneak up on you.

Quick answer

The best long-distance anniversary has two parts: one thing you do at the same time (a video dinner, a "rewind" call, a shared movie) and one thing that shows up in their hands or on their phone (a letter, a delivery, a drawing). The live part gives you togetherness. The physical or sent part gives them something to keep. Add a date on the calendar for when you'll celebrate in person, and the day stops feeling like a consolation prize.

Free or nearly free

  1. The rewind call. Each of you brings three memories from the year: the funniest, the sweetest, and one that only seemed bad at the time. Take turns. It sounds corny, and it's the one people remember.
  2. A letter, on paper. Handwritten, posted a week early so it lands on the day. Short is fine. Four honest lines beat a page of greeting-card filler. If you need a spark, our short love notes list has plenty.
  3. A playlist of your year. One song per month you've been together, with a one-line reason for each. Swap them and listen on the call.
  4. An anniversary doodle. Draw the first place you met, the worst date you survived, or a very small version of the two of you. No artistic skill required; bad drawings are part of the charm. More prompts in our things to doodle for your partner.

A little planning, a little money

  1. Synchronized dinner. Order from the same cuisine (or cook the same recipe), set up the call, and eat "together". Dress up. Light a candle. The ritual is the point, not the food.
  2. The same movie, a real date night. Choose something from your early days. Start it at the same second and keep the call open. Our long-distance date ideas list has more formats if movies aren't your thing.
  3. An "open when" box. Mail a small box with envelopes marked "open when you miss me", "open when you've had a bad day", and "open on our anniversary". Sealed notes work well because you can't peek early.
  4. A surprise delivery. Flowers, their favourite dessert, a coffee from a local shop. Check delivery windows against their time zone, otherwise the ice cream arrives at 3 a.m. For gift ideas that travel well, see our gift guide.

Go bigger

  1. A shared "year in review" album. Pull together twelve photos, one per month, with captions. Send it as a link, or print it and mail it.
  2. Plan the next visit as the gift. Book a date, even a tentative one, and give it to them on the day. A countdown turns the gift into something you both look at daily; see our countdown guide.
  3. A themed "first date" recreation. Same drinks, same playlist, same silly outfit. Ask the questions you asked on the first date, and notice how different the answers feel now. Our question list can fill gaps.
  4. Meet in the middle. If your budget and calendars allow, a weekend somewhere between you. It's the priciest option here and often the best, but it's a bonus, not a requirement. Skip the guilt if the answer this year is no.

A simple plan so the day doesn't sneak up on you

Two weeks out: pick one live idea and one sent idea. One week out: mail anything physical (letters and boxes need time). The day before: check time zones and agree on the exact hour you'll connect. The day: do the live thing first, then reveal the sent thing. After: put the in-person date on the calendar while the warm feeling is fresh.

If your schedules barely overlap, remember that the day can last 24 hours. Sending something in the morning and calling in the evening works fine; our time zone guide covers the awkward overlaps.

FAQ

How do you celebrate an anniversary in a long-distance relationship?

Pick one shared moment on the day (a video date, a synchronized dinner, a call) and add one thing that arrives physically or on their phone, like a letter, a delivery or a doodle. Together they cover 'we did something at the same time' and 'I made you something'.

What if we can't see each other on our anniversary?

Celebrate on the day with something live, then plan the in-person celebration for when you next meet and say so out loud. A date on the calendar turns 'we missed it' into 'we're doing it twice'.

What is a cheap long-distance anniversary idea?

A 'rewind' call where you each bring three memories, a handwritten letter, a shared playlist of your year, or a drawing sent to their phone. None of these need more than a stamp or nothing at all.
