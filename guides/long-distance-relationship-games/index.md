# Long-Distance Relationship Games: 12 Ideas for Video Calls and Phones

By the Twoodle team · November 2026

> **Disclosure:** we make Twoodle, a couples widget app for iPhone. Most of these games need nothing but a phone and a person you like.

Picture this: a video call, two tired faces, and the sacred question. "So... what did you do today?" "Work. You?" "Also work."

That call doesn't need a better conversation. It needs a game. A game gives you something to do with your hands and a reason to laugh, and it takes the pressure off "being interesting."

Below are twelve that work across screens, sorted by how much effort they take. Pick one, send it to your partner, and see who sulks first when they lose.

## Quick answer: what to play first

If you want a game in the next two minutes with zero setup, start with **Would You Rather** or **Two Truths and a Lie** on a video call. If your schedules don't line up, play a daily puzzle and compare scores, or trade doodles for the other person to guess.

## Games for a video call (no setup)

1. **Twenty Questions.** One of you thinks of something, the other has twenty yes-or-no questions. Pick categories that are your own: "something in my flat," "a person from my school."
2. **Two Truths and a Lie.** Better than it sounds when you've been together for years, because you'll find out things you didn't know. Or catch the lie too fast and lose.
3. **Would You Rather.** Start silly ("fight one horse-sized duck?"), end up in real conversation about where you'd live. It's a warm-up for our [long-distance relationship questions](https://twoodle.app/guides/long-distance-relationship-questions/).
4. **Pen-and-paper Pictionary.** Draw on paper, hold it up to the camera, the other person guesses. Stick figures are fully legal.
5. **Show-and-tell.** Each of you gets a minute to show one object in your room and explain why it matters. Sounds dull, always isn't.

## Games that need a shared screen or a link

1. **Browser party games.** Some party-game packs let one person host and others join from a phone browser, which can work if one of you shares their screen. Player counts and prices vary, so check the game's own page before you buy.
2. **Online board games and chess.** Many board games have free online versions you can play in separate tabs while you talk. Voice call on, strategy face on.
3. **Co-op video games.** If you both game, a co-op game beats a competitive one: you're on the same team, and nobody has to apologise for winning.

## Games for when you can't be on a call

1. **Daily word puzzle scoreboard.** Play the same daily puzzle in your own time zones and send your results. Low effort, surprisingly competitive.
2. **Photo scavenger hunt.** One of you sends a clue ("something blue that isn't a shirt"), the other replies with a photo. Switch roles tomorrow.
3. **Doodle and guess.** Draw something in your app, send it, and let your partner guess what it is when they see it. It's the best game for time zones because the game waits.
4. **Finish my drawing.** Send a half-drawn doodle, and your partner completes it and sends it back. Funny result guaranteed.

## Make it stick

- **Keep it short.** Fifteen to thirty minutes. Stop while it's fun.
- **Keep score lightly.** A running tally with a silly prize ("loser sends a voice note of a song") beats serious stakes.
- **Take turns choosing.** The person who picks last time doesn't pick this time.
- **Mind the clock.** If you're a long way apart, our [time zone guide](https://twoodle.app/guides/long-distance-relationship-time-zones/) helps you find a slot that works.
- **Don't replace talking.** Games are a bridge to conversation, not a substitute. How much contact is right? See [how often long-distance couples should talk](https://twoodle.app/guides/how-often-should-long-distance-couples-talk/).

## FAQ

### What are good games for long-distance couples?

The easiest ones need nothing but a video call: Twenty Questions, Two Truths and a Lie, Would You Rather, and drawing games where one of you sketches on paper and the other guesses. For days when you can't be on a call, try a shared daily word puzzle, a photo scavenger hunt or doodle games sent to a widget.

### What can we play together if we can't be on a call at the same time?

Play asynchronously: compare scores on a daily puzzle, send one photo clue at a time, or send a doodle and let your partner guess when they see it. Time zones stop mattering when the game waits for you.

### How long should a long-distance game session last?

Fifteen to thirty minutes is plenty. End while it's still fun, not when somebody is yawning. A short game that happens often beats a three-hour marathon that happens once.
