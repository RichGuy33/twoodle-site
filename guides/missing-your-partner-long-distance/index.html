Missing Your Partner in a Long-Distance Relationship: 12 Things That Actually Help

By the Twoodle team · November 2026

Disclosure: we make Twoodle, a couples widget app for iPhone. Most of these ideas need nothing but a phone and a person you miss.

It's 9:40 on a Tuesday. Nothing is wrong. You just walked past a cafe that sells the pastry they like, and now your chest feels like someone sat on it.

You can't hug a screen, and "stay positive!" is not a plan. So here's something more useful: twelve small things that help in the exact moment you miss your partner, sorted by what you're feeling.

Quick answer: what helps right now

Send them something small and specific (a two-line message, a photo of the pastry, a doodle), then do something with your body for ten minutes. Missing someone is partly a feeling that wants to go somewhere. Sending it to them and moving around usually works better than sitting still and replaying it.

When you just want a connection, fast

  1. Send a "thinking of you" with a detail. "Saw your pastry. Hated it. Wanted you." beats "miss you" because it proves you were paying attention.
  2. Send a doodle. A bad drawing says "I thought about you" without any pressure to reply. Our list of things to doodle for your partner has ideas for when your mind is blank.
  3. Send a voice note. Ten seconds of your actual voice does more than a paragraph. They can play it twice.

When it hits at night

  1. Make a bedtime ritual. Same three things every night: a message, a song, a "sleep well." Rituals give a lonely hour a shape, and you don't have to be on a call to share one. For ten options, see our long-distance goodnight routine guide.
  2. Write it down first. Two minutes in your notes app: what you miss, what you'll tell them. Half the weight is just having nowhere to put the thought.
  3. Check the clock before the spiral. If it's the middle of their night, a message can wait. A scheduled plan for tomorrow beats a 3 a.m. text that wakes them up. Our time zone guide helps you find a slot that works for both of you.

When the missing is a weekend-sized hole

  1. Plan the next visit, even roughly. A date on the calendar changes missing from "when?" to "how many days?" See our countdown until we meet guide for ways to make the wait visible.
  2. Do something you'd do together, apart. Same recipe, same show, same walk, on a call or not. It's less sad than it sounds.
  3. Fill the weekend on purpose. Friends, a class, a long run. Missing someone is louder in an empty schedule.

When you need to actually talk about it

  1. Say "I'm having a missing-you day." It's not a complaint. It gives your partner something to answer, and they probably have days like it too.
  2. Ask for what you want. "Can we do a ten-minute call tonight?" is clearer than hoping they notice. Not sure how much contact is right? Read how often long-distance couples should talk.
  3. Check in on the bigger picture. If missing has become constant and heavy, talk about the plan: end dates, visits, and who is moving where. Our long-distance relationship questions include a set for that conversation.

One thing not to do

Don't use missing them as a test. If you're hoping they'll "just know," tell them. Most missing-you days are fixed by one clear sentence and one small thing sent in return.

If the sadness feels heavy, constant or starts getting in the way of work, sleep or daily life, it's worth talking to someone you trust or a mental health professional. We're an app team, not therapists, and this guide is not medical advice.

FAQ

Is it normal to miss your partner this much in a long-distance relationship?

Yes. Missing someone you love who lives far away is a normal part of being apart, not a sign that something is wrong. It tends to come in waves, often around quiet moments, weekends and goodbyes.

What should I do when I miss my long-distance partner at night?

Keep it small and doable: send a short message or doodle instead of a long one, do something physical like a shower or a walk, and agree on a tiny bedtime ritual with your partner so the night has a shape.

Should I tell my partner when I'm feeling low about the distance?

Usually yes, in a calm moment and without turning it into blame. Saying "I'm having a missing-you day" gives your partner something to respond to, while hiding it tends to leave both of you guessing.
