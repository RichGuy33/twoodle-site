Long-Distance Relationship Questions: 30 to Ask (and When to Ask Them)

By the Twoodle team · November 2026

Disclosure: we make Twoodle, an iPhone app for couples. Every question below works with no app at all, and we say where Twoodle fits.

Short answer: the best long-distance questions are small, specific and about their actual day. Save the big ones for a proper call. Below are 30, sorted by the moment you'd really ask them.

Search for this topic and you'll find lists of 286, 510, even more. Nobody has ever asked their partner question 347. We picked 30 and told you when each one works.

Why does timing matter? Because "What are your fears about our future?" lands very differently at 7 a.m. while they're hunting for socks than on a quiet Sunday call. Same question, different relationship outcome.

Daily check-ins: light, fast, answerable in one line

These fit in a text or a voice note. The goal isn't depth. It's proving you're curious about the ordinary parts of their day, the part you can't see.

  • What was the best five minutes of your day?
  • What did you eat, and was it good or just fuel?
  • Who was the most interesting person you talked to today?
  • What's one thing that annoyed you that I should know about?
  • What are you looking forward to this week?
  • What song is stuck in your head?

Weekly call questions: a bit deeper, still easy

Pick one per call, not five. A single good question with room to breathe beats a rapid-fire quiz.

  • What's something you changed your mind about recently?
  • What's a small thing I do that makes your week better?
  • What would your perfect ordinary Saturday with me look like?
  • What's the most stressful thing on your plate right now, and how can I help?
  • What's a memory of us you replay?
  • If we had a free hour together tomorrow, what would you pick?

Fun questions: for when the conversation goes flat

Every long-distance couple hits a night where "how was your day" gets "fine" three times in a row. Try these. They work because they're silly enough to answer without effort.

  • Which of our friends would survive longest in a zombie film?
  • What would your autobiography be called?
  • If we swapped cities for a week, what would you do first in mine?
  • What's your most unreasonable food opinion?
  • What's the worst gift you've ever received?
  • Which two songs would be on our couple soundtrack?

Relationship questions: when you want to go deeper

Ask these when you both have energy and time, not when one of you is on the way to a meeting. A good test: would you be okay if the conversation ran an hour?

  • What makes you feel most loved by me, in a normal week?
  • When do you feel the distance the most?
  • What do you need more of from me, and what do you need less of?
  • What does a good week of staying in touch look like to you?
  • What's something you're afraid to ask me?
  • What do we do when one of us is having a bad day and can't talk?

Future questions: the closing-the-distance conversation

These are the ones that make long-distance relationships feel finite. They matter, and they can be tense, so go in kindly and don't demand final answers in one sitting.

  • Where do we each picture living in two years?
  • What would need to be true for one of us to move?
  • What's our next visit, and who is booking it?
  • What would make you feel more secure about the plan?
  • What are we each willing to compromise on, and what's non-negotiable?
  • How will we know when it's time to set a date for closing the distance?

How to ask questions without it feeling like an interview

  • One at a time. A list of five questions is a form. One question is a conversation.
  • Answer first. Say your own answer or offer it right after theirs. Otherwise it's surveillance with better lighting.
  • Mind the time zone. A heavy question sent just before their meeting or your bedtime backfires. Our time zones guide covers finding a fair window.
  • Let silence happen. Video calls make a three-second pause feel like a crash. It isn't. They're thinking.
  • Follow up later. Referencing an answer from last week shows you actually listened. That's what makes the question worth asking.

FAQ

What are good questions to ask your long-distance partner?

Good questions are specific and about their real day: what was the best part of today, what are you looking forward to this week, what did you eat. Save deeper questions about the future, money or how the distance feels for a proper video call when you both have time.

How often should long-distance couples ask deep questions?

Once a week is plenty for most couples. Keep the daily check-in light and put one deeper question on your weekly call so it does not feel like an interview every night.

What should I ask my long-distance partner when we run out of things to talk about?

Ask about small, concrete things: something that annoyed them today, something they saw that reminded them of you, or what they would do with a free Saturday. If conversation feels thin, change the format with a voice note, a shared activity or a doodle instead of another text.
