# Best Apps for Long-Distance Relationships (A Practical Setup)

By the Twoodle team · November 2026

> **Disclosure:** we make Twoodle, so one of the apps below is ours. We kept it to one slot out of six, and we say where it doesn't help.

Every "best long-distance apps" list has the same flaw: it pretends one app will save your relationship.

It won't. Distance isn't an app problem. It's a "you're asleep while I'm eating lunch" problem.

Short answer: you need a small kit, not a winner. One app to talk, one to see each other's face, one to keep time zones straight (more in our [time zones guide](https://twoodle.app/guides/long-distance-relationship-time-zones/)), one to watch things together, and one tiny thing that says "I'm thinking of you" without needing a whole conversation. Here's that kit, with the honest limits of each.

## The six jobs (and one app for each)

| Job | A solid pick | Worth knowing |
|---|---|---|
| Everyday messaging | iMessage (if you're both on iPhone) or WhatsApp | Pick one thread and live there. Splitting chat across three apps is how messages get lost. |
| Video calls | FaceTime | Apple devices only; if one of you is on Android, use a cross-platform call app instead. |
| Time zones | The Clock app's World Clock | Built into iPhone. Add your partner's city and stop doing mental math. |
| Watching together | FaceTime SharePlay | Works with supported apps; some streaming services also have their own watch-party tools. Check yours. |
| Shared memories | Shared Albums in Photos | Free and built in. A shared album for "things we saw this week" is surprisingly lovely. |
| Small daily presence | A shared widget app, such as Twoodle | See below. Not a replacement for talking. |

Notice what's missing: a big "all-in-one couples app." Some people love those. Many find they use one feature and ignore the rest. Start with what you already have on your phone, then add only what you actually miss.

## Messaging: pick one thread

If you both use iPhone, iMessage is the lowest-effort option. If one of you doesn't, WhatsApp is a common cross-platform choice. The rule that matters more than the app: keep your main conversation in one place, so there's one scroll to search when you want to find that restaurant name.

## Video: schedule it, but not too much

FaceTime is the simplest on Apple devices. Many couples find a fixed call time helps, as long as it doesn't turn into a meeting you're being graded on. Some nights, just leaving the call open while you both do chores is the better date.

## Time zones: the quiet relationship saver

Open Clock, tap World Clock, and add your partner's city. That's the whole trick. You'll stop waking them up and start noticing when it's their lunch break, which is a perfect moment to send something small.

## Watching and listening together

FaceTime's SharePlay lets you watch or listen together during a call in supported apps. Support varies by app and service, so test your favorite before date night, not during it. A shared playlist is a lower-tech version that costs nothing.

If you talk daily, call weekly and feel connected, you're done. Don't add apps for the sake of it. The best setup is the one you both actually open. Two apps used happily beats seven abandoned.

## FAQ

### What is the best app for a long-distance relationship?

There isn't one. Most couples do well with a messaging app, a video-call app, a World Clock for time zones, a way to watch together, and a small daily-presence app.

### How can we watch movies together on iPhone?

FaceTime SharePlay works in supported apps. Some streaming services have their own watch-party features, so check each one.
